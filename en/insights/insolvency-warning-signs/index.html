Insolvency proceedings: five signs to act in time

When to review with a lawyer and a financial advisor whether a company is facing insolvency, and why acting early keeps more options open.

Published on October 5, 2026. Practice area: Corporate and Finance

A company rarely becomes insolvent overnight. There are usually earlier signs that, when addressed in time, open more paths: negotiating with creditors, restructuring debt or, if needed, filing for insolvency proceedings (concurso mercantil) with a plan.

Five signs worth reviewing

  1. Payments to suppliers, banks or authorities that keep being deferred.
  2. Short-term credit used to cover operating expenses.
  3. Demands or attachments that begin to pile up.
  4. Cash flow that falls short of the month’s payroll or taxes.
  5. Creditors asking for additional guarantees or changing their terms.

Why review it with a lawyer and a financial advisor

The decision is neither only legal nor only financial. A joint assessment shows which debts can be negotiated, which are better addressed within a proceeding, and which business plan makes the company viable.
